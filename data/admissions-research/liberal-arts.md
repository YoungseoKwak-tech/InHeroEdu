# Top liberal-arts colleges — admit benchmarks & fit analysis

*Stream: `liberal-arts` · Generated 2026-06-24 · 12 schools*

This analysis covers twelve of the most selective U.S. liberal-arts colleges (LACs):
Williams, Amherst, Swarthmore, Pomona, Bowdoin, Wellesley, Carleton, Claremont
McKenna, Middlebury, Davidson, Vassar, and Colby. Structured figures live in
`liberal-arts.json`; every numeric data point there carries a source URL. This file
summarizes the patterns a parent or counselor should understand.

## At-a-glance admit-rate gradient

Despite near-identical academic profiles, these schools span a wide selectivity range
— useful for building a balanced reach/target list:

## The four things that actually matter

**1. Everyone is test-optional — and the published score bands lie a little.**
All twelve are currently test-optional (none test-required, test-blind, or test-free).
Crucially, only a minority of admits submit scores (Williams ~35%, Swarthmore ~39%;
~44% of Middlebury's admitted Class of 2029 sent no scores at all). That means the
reported SAT/ACT 25th–75th bands describe a *self-selected* high-scoring subset and
**overstate** what a typical applicant needs. Treat them as "if you submit, aim here,"
not "you must hit this." One school to watch: **Claremont McKenna** faculty voted to
reinstate testing, intended for fall 2028 (Class of 2032) — so it stays optional for
current cycles.

**2. There is effectively no GPA number to chase.**
None of these colleges publishes a mean high-school GPA in its Common Data Set. They
report **class rank**, and the answer is uniformly "top 10% of class" (Wellesley 90%,
Bowdoin ~85%, Colby ~86%, CMC ~75%, Davidson ~68% top-decile; Davidson also shows
~40% of enrolled students at a 4.0). Any specific GPA figure you see online (~3.9) is an
aggregator estimate, not an official statistic.

**3. Test scores are a threshold, not a differentiator.**
The bands are remarkably tight across the whole set — SAT 25th roughly 1450–1500,
75th roughly 1530–1560; ACT 25th 31–34, 75th 34–35. Once a student clears the
threshold, additional points buy almost nothing. **Fit and character are the real
selection axis** at this tier.

**4. EC philosophy is identical everywhere: depth over breadth.**
Across all twelve the valued pattern is the same — sustained, deep commitment to one or
two areas, demonstrated leadership and tangible impact, and authentic civic/community
engagement that aligns with the school's stated mission. A long, shallow resume is the
anti-pattern.

## Where they genuinely differ: fit archetypes

The differentiation is structural and cultural, and it maps cleanly onto distinct
applicant types. This is the part worth coaching a student toward:

- **Williams** — Oxford-style tutorials (2 students, 1 professor) + a central
  scholar-athlete identity. For students who want to argue and defend ideas in tiny
  rooms and who thrive in a strong athletics culture.
- **Amherst** & **Vassar** — open curriculum (no requirements). For self-directed
  students who can justify their own academic path; Vassar adds a strong arts/creative,
  progressive flavor.
- **Swarthmore** — graduate-level intensity + an earnest, non-competitive, Quaker
  social-justice ethos. For intellectually serious students who love hard questions.
- **Pomona** — small-college intimacy with Claremont-consortium breadth (~2,700
  cross-registerable classes) and a notably happy, collaborative culture.
- **Bowdoin** — organized around "the common good." For mission-driven, service-oriented
  students; a long-standing test-optional pioneer (since 1969).
- **Wellesley** — women's leadership and empowerment in a rigorous, tight-knit setting.
- **Carleton** — quirky, collaborative, intellectually intense (trimester system,
  strong quant/STEM); the supplement explicitly invites personality.
- **Claremont McKenna** — pre-professional econ/government/public-affairs and leadership
  (Robert Day School, applied research institutes). For future policy, finance, and
  public-service leaders.
- **Davidson** — built around a student-run Honor Code (unproctored, take-home exams).
  For students whose integrity and community values are central.
- **Middlebury** — environmental studies, the renowned Language Schools, and an
  outdoor/civic culture in rural Vermont; demonstrated interest and fit matter.
- **Colby** — "intellectually adventurous" students; the DavisConnects model funds
  research, internships, and global experiences for everyone.

## Sourcing caveats (important for a parent-facing feature)

- **Cite the cycle per school.** The freshest *admit rate* is often newer than the
  freshest published *score distribution*. Middlebury and Davidson admit rates are
  Class of 2029 while their score/GPA bands trace to the Class of 2028 CDS; Colby's
  headline is Class of 2030.
- **Colby publishes no Common Data Set** (none since 2016). Its figures come only from
  official news releases and the admissions college profile — inherently less granular
  and harder to verify than the CDS-backed schools.
- **Carleton's Class-of-2029 admit rate (21.78%) is third-party** (Expert Admissions),
  because that cycle's CDS isn't out yet. Its score bands are from the 2023-2024 CDS.
- Where a college's CDS leaves the median (p50) blank, the JSON sets `p50: null` rather
  than inventing one. A few "median" figures elsewhere are reported *averages* — noted
  per school.
- GPA figures are deliberately omitted as numbers and replaced with class-rank notes,
  because no school in this set publishes an official mean GPA.

Primary sources are each college's official Common Data Set / Office of Institutional
Research, supplemented by official admissions pages and news releases, with reputable
aggregators (PrepScholar, CollegeVine, Niche, commondatasets.fyi) only as secondary
corroboration. Reddit was not used.
