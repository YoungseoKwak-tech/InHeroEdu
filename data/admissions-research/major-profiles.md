# Major profiles — competitive applicant by intended field

*Stream: `major-profiles` · Generated 2026-06-24*

What a competitive applicant looks like by intended major/field, and — critically —
**when applying to a major actually raises the bar**. The single most important idea
below is that this depends on each school's *admissions structure*, not on the field's
prestige.

> **Sourcing & caveats.** Most colleges do **not** publish per-major admit rates in their
> Common Data Sets, so field-level "acceptance rate" figures are directional and attributed
> to the aggregators that estimate them. Structural facts (admit-by-major vs. holistic) are
> confirmed against the colleges' own pages where possible. Olympiad admit-rate figures are
> **correlations**, not formulas — qualifiers are exceptional across their whole application.
> Nothing here is fabricated; unverifiable numbers are described qualitatively.

---

## The one rule that governs everything: read the school's structure

| Structure | Effect of declaring the major | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Admit-by-major** | Your declared major is its own, harder pool; alternates often not reviewed | UC Berkeley **EECS**, Wharton (UPenn) |
| **Admit-by-school (major non-binding)** | You're admitted to a selective *school*, but the dept you list doesn't factor in | **CMU School of Computer Science** |
| **Holistic, major chosen later** | Stating interest does **not** change your odds | MIT, Stanford, most Ivies |

A common trap: people assume "applying CS at CMU" puts you in a CS pool. In fact CMU admits
to the **School of Computer Science as a whole**, and the *department* you list is used only
for post-admit outreach — but SCS is itself far more selective than CMU's other colleges.

## 1. Computer Science

- **Context:** The most oversubscribed field everywhere, but the mechanism differs. Berkeley
  admits first-years **directly into EECS** (alternate major not reviewed). CMU admits to SCS
  as a whole. MIT/Stanford are holistic. So "should I apply as CS?" has a different answer at
  each school.
- **Coursework:** Calc (AB/BC) → multivariable/linear algebra if available; AP CS A and beyond
  (data structures, algorithms); AP Physics (C at engineering-housed programs).
- **EC signals:** USACO standing (Silver/Gold/Platinum), shipped projects / open-source,
  hackathon wins, university research. Depth over breadth.
- **Notable:** MIT, Stanford, CMU SCS (≈<5% pool), Berkeley EECS, Georgia Tech Computing.

## 2. Engineering

- **Context:** Usually admit-by-college/major, so judged against engineering-specific
  expectations. Pure institutes (MIT, **Caltech ≈2-3%**) are STEM-saturated; at Georgia Tech
  the College of Engineering is among the hardest divisions and **OOS (~9%)** is far below the
  overall (~15%).
- **Coursework:** Hardest math/science available — calculus required, multivariable/linear
  algebra/diff-eq a plus; AP Physics C (Mech + E&M), AP Chem; near-4.0 unweighted at Caltech.
- **EC signals:** **Original research** (Caltech: ~45% of admits did independent research;
  publication/competition validation > data-collection internships), robotics (FIRST), Science
  Olympiad, national competition placement.
- **Notable:** MIT, Caltech, Georgia Tech, Berkeley Engineering, Stanford.

## 3. Biology / Pre-med

- **Context:** **Pre-med is not a major** — it's a prereq set alongside any major, so it rarely
  changes undergrad odds the way restricted CS/engineering does. The signal is rigor + a
  coherent clinical/research record. JHU admit profile ≈ **3.95+ UW GPA, 8-10+ AP/IB-HL/post-AP**.
- **Coursework:** AP Bio, AP Chem, AP Physics (skipping these "raises questions" at JHU);
  calculus; strong writing. College science is often curved (As to top ~10-15%).
- **EC signals:** Sustained biomedical research (ideally a publication), clinical exposure
  (JHU admits cited with **300+ hospital hours**), physician shadowing; depth in 2-3 areas.
- **Notable:** Johns Hopkins (~6%; Neuroscience, BME, MCB, Public Health dominate pre-med),
  Harvard, Stanford, Duke, WashU; plus selective **BS/MD** combined programs.

## 4. Business / Economics

- **Context:** Sharply admit-by-school at the top: **Wharton admits separately** and sits below
  UPenn's ~6.5% overall (~93.5% of UPenn applicants rejected in 2024-25). Coordinated dual
  degrees (Penn **Huntsman**) require freshman application and are more selective still. Many
  **economics** programs, by contrast, live in arts & sciences and are *not* admit-by-major.
- **Coursework:** AP Calc (BC), AP Statistics, AP Econ (Micro/Macro); top GPA (UPenn admit avg
  ~3.9, SAT ~1510-1560); quantitative strength read closely for econ/finance.
- **EC signals:** DECA/FBLA finalists (ICDC), Wharton Global High School Investment Competition,
  real entrepreneurship (Diamond/Conrad Challenge, a venture with traction), team leadership.
- **Notable:** Wharton/UPenn, MIT Sloan & MIT Economics, Harvard Economics, Columbia, NYU Stern.

## 5. Humanities

- **Context:** Almost always holistic (arts & sciences / undeclared), so the intended major
  rarely raises the bar mechanically — the bar is **qualitative**: demonstrated intellectual
  depth and writing. Yale weights intellectual curiosity; Princeton is research-intensive
  (mandatory **year-long senior thesis** for A.B. students). Harvard/Princeton ≈ 3-4% overall.
- **Coursework:** AP English Lit + Lang; AP US/European/World History; at least one AP world
  language; top GPA; SAT ~1500+.
- **EC signals:** Serious writing/research — **The Concord Review** (history) is a recognized
  admissions signal (cited by a former Harvard dean of admissions); selective writing programs,
  high-level debate/Model UN, national writing awards. A humanistic "spike" over scattered clubs.
- **Notable:** Yale, Princeton, Harvard, Brown, Columbia.

## 6. Physics / Math

- **Context:** Olympiad achievement carries outsized weight at technical institutes. Aggregator
  analyses report MIT **USAMO qualifiers admitted >50%** vs. <~7% overall, and **U.S. Physics
  Team (Top 24) >60%** at MIT/Caltech; USAPhO Semifinalist status alone is associated with
  elevated odds. (Correlations — qualifiers are exceptional all around.)
- **Coursework:** Calc BC + post-AP math (multivariable, linear algebra, proof-based); AP Physics
  C, often early; university courses / advanced self-study; top STEM grades.
- **EC signals:** Math — AMC → AIME → **USAMO**; Physics — F=ma → **USAPhO Semifinalist** →
  **U.S. Physics Team**; research that amplifies the competition signal; deep, sustained
  problem-solving.
- **Notable:** MIT, Caltech, Princeton, Harvey Mudd, Stanford.

## Cross-field insights

1. **Structure, not prestige, decides whether a major raises the bar.** Admit-by-major schools
   (Berkeley EECS, Wharton) penalize an over-subscribed declaration; holistic schools (MIT,
   Stanford, most Ivies) don't.
2. **Always check the mechanism.** CMU admits to SCS as a whole; the department you list is
   non-binding — yet SCS is a much harder pool than CMU's other colleges.
3. **Pre-med ≠ a major.** Build the signal through rigor (AP Bio/Chem/Physics), research, and
   clinical hours, not through the label.
4. **External validation is the strongest lever, and it's field-specific:** USACO/USAMO (CS,
   math), USAPhO/U.S. Physics Team (physics), The Concord Review (humanities), DECA/FBLA/
   investment competitions (business), original research (engineering, pre-med).
5. **Depth beats breadth everywhere** — one or two sustained, validated "spikes."
6. **Olympiad admit rates are evidence of weighting, not a formula.** Treat MIT's "USAMO >50%"
   as how much the signal is valued, not a guarantee.
