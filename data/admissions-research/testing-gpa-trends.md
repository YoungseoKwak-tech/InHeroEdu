## The headline: required testing is back at the top

For the 2024-2025 and 2025-2026 cycles, the test-optional era effectively ended at
the most selective tier. The reinstatement timeline:

- **MIT** — first mover, announced **March 2022** ([MIT Admissions](https://mitadmissions.org/blogs/entry/we-are-reinstating-our-sat-act-requirement-for-future-admissions-cycles/)).
- **Dartmouth & Yale** — announced **Feb 2024**; **Brown** followed **March 2024**.
- **Harvard** — announced **April 11, 2024**, effective for Fall 2025 applicants / Class of 2029 ([Harvard Gazette](https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2024/04/harvard-announces-return-to-required-testing/)).
- **Caltech** — ended its test-blind moratorium **April 2024**, required for Fall 2025 entry ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/caltech-restores-standardized-test-requirement-for-undergraduate-admission)).
- **Johns Hopkins** — required from Fall 2025 ([Applerouth](https://www.applerouth.com/blog/johns-hopkins-reinstates-sat-act-requirements)).
- **UT Austin** — major public flagship, required from Fall 2025 ([UT Austin News](https://news.utexas.edu/2024/03/11/ut-austin-reinstates-standardized-test-scores-in-admissions/)).
- **Stanford** — required starting one cycle later, **Class of 2030** (Fall 2025 applicants); test-optional for the Class of 2029 ([Crimson](https://www.crimsoneducation.org/us/blog/colleges-that-require-sat-act)).

### Three policy nuances that matter

1. **"Required" is not uniform.** **Yale is test-FLEXIBLE** — it accepts SAT, ACT,
   AP, *or* IB scores ([Yale Admissions](https://admissions.yale.edu/standardized-testing)). Most others want SAT or ACT specifically,
   usually with narrow hardship exceptions.
2. **Georgetown never really left** — it has long required SAT/ACT for all
   applicants and uses its own (non–Common App) application ([Georgetown Admissions](https://uadmissions.georgetown.edu/applying/transfer-test-scores/)).
3. **The University of California is the big outlier — test-FREE.** The entire UC
   system (Berkeley, UCLA, UCSD, UCI, UCSB, UCD, UCR, UCSC, Merced) has been
   **test-blind since 2021**: SAT/ACT scores are *not considered at all*
   ([UC press release](https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/press-room/university-california-board-regents-unanimously-approved-changes-standardized-testing)).
   BOARS began re-examining this in late 2024 / early 2025 over grade-inflation and
   math-readiness concerns, but the test-free policy is still in force.

## How GPA actually appears in the Common Data Set

A recurring misconception: there is **no single "average GPA"** in the CDS. Section
**C11/C12** reports high-school GPA in **bands** — % of enrolled first-years with a
4.0, 3.75–3.99, 3.50–3.74, and so on — plus class-rank bands. At the most selective
schools the top band is saturated:

- **Johns Hopkins:** **70%+** of enrolled first-years had a **4.0**, and **90%+**
  had **3.75 or higher** ([JHU CDS 2024-25](https://oira.jhu.edu/wp-content/uploads/CDS_2024-2025_JHU.pdf)).
- **MIT:** of the ~28% who reported class rank, **~99%** were in the top tenth
  ([MIT CDS 2024-25](https://ir.mit.edu/projects/2024-25-common-data-set/)).
- **Yale:** **96%** of admitted students were in the top tenth of their class
  ([Yale OIR CDS](https://oir.yale.edu/common-data-set)).

Because the top band is so crowded, a 4.0 no longer differentiates applicants — which
feeds directly into why scores came back.

## Grade-inflation context (the "why" behind reinstatement)

Schools that reinstated cite internal research that test scores predict first-year
performance better than grades *when grading is inflated*. The macro data backs this:

- Average high-school GPA rose from **3.22 (2010)** to **3.39 (2021)** even as ACT
  scores **fell** over the same window ([ACT](https://leadershipblog.act.org/2023/08/grade-inflation-math.html)).
- A top-25% test-taker (ACT 25) had an average GPA of **3.5 in 2010** but **3.7 in
  2021** — same achievement, higher grades ([Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-new-evidence-of-high-school-grade-inflation/)).
- The share of students reporting "A" grades rose ~10–12 percentage points across
  English, math, social studies, and science from 2010 to 2022, with inflation
  worst in math.

The admissions takeaway, stated by Dartmouth, MIT, Brown, and Harvard: with grades
compressed near the ceiling and varying by high school, a standardized score lets
offices compare applicants on a common scale — and helps surface strong students at
under-resourced schools.

## Caveats

- Several "mid-50%" figures here come from reputable aggregators rather than a
  directly-parsed CDS PDF; admitted-vs-enrolled and reporting year can shift a
  range by 10–20 points. Treat them as directional.
- A few official CDS GPA/SAT cells (e.g., MIT composite mid-50%, Dartmouth/Brown
  exact enrolled bands) were left `null` rather than approximated. Pull the primary
  CDS PDFs to fill these precisely before showing them to families.
