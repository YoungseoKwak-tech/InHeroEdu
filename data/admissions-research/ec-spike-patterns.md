# EC & Award Spike Patterns — what elite admits actually do

This note summarizes the extracurricular and award patterns associated with admits at the
most selective US colleges. It powers the `/parents` "합격생 스펙·에세이 분석" feature, so
every claim below is tied to a reputable source (college statements, well-known counseling

## 1. Spike vs. well-rounded

The dominant framework: build a **spike** (one area of deep, distinctive excellence) rather
than a **well-rounded** profile (broad but shallow). Selective schools assemble a *class* of
specialists, so a spike gives an admissions officer a one-line hook to champion the file in
committee — "did a lot of different things" is not a hook. Harvard's own criteria foreground
"leadership and distinction in extracurricular activities," and counselors are blunt that at
the top, "well-rounded" reads as "not distinctive."

## 2. The activities-tier idea (level matters more than label)

Counselors commonly sort activities into ~4 tiers by *achieved level*:

- **Tier 1** — rare national/international distinction (USAMO win, IBO/IMO selection, major
  science awards, founding an org with real reach and press coverage).
- **Tier 2** — state/regional achievement or leadership (state champion, statewide org officer).
- **Tier 3** — school/community engagement (club president, paper editor, sustained service).
- **Tier 4** — hobbies and general participation.

The key is that tier tracks **rarity and verifiability** (international > national > state >
school), not a title. A "president" of an inactive club sits below a member who drove a real
outcome.

## 3. High-signal awards & programs

- **Academic olympiads.** The AMC 10/12 → AIME → USAMO ladder (only ~500 in the US reach
  USAMO) and the IMO, plus USABO/IBO, physics/chem, and USACO. Externally verified and
  extremely selective; counselors describe medalists being flagged like recruited athletes.
- **Selective research programs.** MIT's **Research Science Institute (RSI)** — ~100 admitted
  from 3,000+ applicants (~2.5–3%), cost-free — verifies advanced original research capacity.
  Note: it verifies ability but does *not* guarantee admission; an RSI admit can still be rejected.
- **ISEF.** Finalists at the Regeneron International Science and Engineering Fair show a level
  of cross-cultural scientific engagement officers rarely see in applicants.
- **Published research — conditionally.** Verifiable, peer-reviewed publication correlates with
  admission (Penn reported ~a third of an admitted class did research; Caltech noted ~45%
  documented research). **But** ProPublica documented a "fast-growing epidemic" of pay-to-publish
  student journals; predatory placements now read as bought and can backfire.
- **Founding an org — for impact, not the act.** Counts for measurable outcomes and longevity,
  not for filing a 501(c)(3). Officers are increasingly skeptical of application-driven nonprofits;
  running several thin ones at once is a red flag.

## 4. Leadership: depth over breadth

Leadership is read as **deepening responsibility and real outcomes** within one or two
commitments — motivating others, deciding, effecting change — not as a stack of titles.
Roughly 72% of admissions officers prefer consistent involvement in one issue over a spread
across many causes.

- College Match Point — [Pitfalls of Resume Padding](https://www.collegematchpoint.com/the-pitfalls-of-resume-padding-in-highly-selective-college-admissions)
- Harvard College — [Admissions criteria FAQ](https://college.harvard.edu/resources/faq/what-admissions-criteria-do-you-use)

## 5. What reads as low-signal

Long lists of passive memberships, "checklist-style" activities pursued briefly, titles with
no contribution, and pay-to-publish/pay-to-found credentials. Officers say they see "right
through" resume padding; five deeply pursued activities beat fifteen superficial ones. MIT even
**caps the application at four activities** — a structural signal that depth, not volume, is read.

## Caveats

- Tier labels and "72%"-style figures come from counseling orgs/surveys, not college
  registrars — treat them as directional, not official admit data.
- Olympiad-medalist "acceptance rate" figures circulate widely but are not published by the
  colleges; they are omitted from the structured data as unsourceable primary statistics.
- High-signal credentials *verify* ability; none *guarantee* admission. Authenticity and real
  impact are the through-line across every pattern here.
